People have long dreamed of an assistant that is always there: one that sees what they see, remembers what they forget, and guides them through what they have never done. Wearable AI is bringing that dream within reach.

Can today’s agents truly help people in the physical world?

InteractiveWorld

The first environment that realistically simulates a human and a wearable AI assistant interacting in the physical world: the assistant sees through the human’s eyes and talks, the human decides and acts, and the world responds.

    Fully Autonomous Planning & Behavior

    The human in InteractiveWorld is an autonomous agent. It hears free-form advice, decides what to do from what it sees, and its body works out the rest: where to stand, how to get there around furniture and through doors, how to lean or squat, and how to grip each object.

    InteractiveBench

    The first closed-loop benchmark for egocentric AI assistants, with 120 multi-turn tasks in six categories. In every task, each instruction shapes what the human does next, and success is read from the world’s final state.

    A Unified Evaluation Framework

    The model under test plays the assistant and sees only the human’s first-person view. A fixed MLLM plays the human and only follows its guidance. InteractiveWorld carries out every action, so the assistant’s words decide the outcome.

              Evaluation Results

              Twelve frontier MLLMs take turns in the assistant’s seat, with Gemini 3.1 Pro as the same human agent for all of them. A task passes only if every goal state holds at the end; MCR also gives credit for partial progress.

              Five Key Findings

              1

              Closed-Loop Assistance Is Far from Solved.

              Even the strongest model helps the human finish fewer than half of the tasks (41.7%), and every other model stays at or below 33.3%. With an oracle assistant, the same human passes all of them.

              2

              One-Shot Decisions Work. Sustaining the Loop Does Not.

              The best models reach 85% on Decision, which often hinges on one scene judgment, but only 20% on Guidance and 25% on Planning, which demand continuous tracking, error spotting and replanning. Success also drops quickly as tasks get longer.

              3

              Most Failures Start with Perception and Memory.

              Misreading the scene or losing track of changes causes 70.4% to 85.7% of first errors. In a closed loop, a single misperception or forgotten state cascades into every instruction that follows.

              4

              Communication Is Critical.

              GLM 5.3 Flash and Claude Fable 5.1 have almost the same average capability score, yet GLM scores far lower on communication and completes 11.7 points fewer tasks. Success is bounded by the weakest link in the chain, not by the average.

              5

              Assistants Waste the Human’s Effort.

              Even on tasks they pass, models lead the human through many unnecessary actions: 2.35× to 3.66× the oracle’s steps on Guidance, and up to 6.75× on Accessibility. Efficiency depends on the task more than on the model.

              InteractiveWorld turns “does this assistant actually help?” into a question with a verifiable answer. Its state-based rewards also open the door to training assistants that truly help people.
